# Act 11.5 — Life in Hell

**Era:** [[Power Unbound (Era 3)]]  
**Previous:** [[Act 11 — Temporary Alliance]]  
**Next:** [[Act 12 — Expansion into Prioxis]]

## Overview

**Life in Hell** is an interlude within [[Power Unbound (Era 3)|Power Unbound]] focused on the soldiers, commanders, machines, families, and everyday culture of the [[Hell Hosts]].

Rather than following a single major war or galactic crisis, the Act consists of fifteen smaller interconnected stories exploring life within one of the largest military powers in the [[Omicron Galaxy]].

Much of the Act takes place on [[Voridinus II]], particularly around [[Vanguard Tower]], its surrounding military installations, barracks, training grounds, hangars, command facilities, and recreational areas.

The Act explores the Hell Hosts outside of major warfare.

Commanders perform inspections and review casualties. Soldiers train, argue, gamble, recover, socialise, and prepare for future deployments. Pilots and mechanics maintain aircraft. Robotic personnel interact with biological soldiers. Military families deal with the expectations created by their names. Some soldiers attempt to enjoy leave while others struggle to separate themselves from their service.

Throughout the Act, the absence of [[Destiny]] remains noticeable.

Although the Hell Hosts continue functioning without her, Victoria remains missing following her abduction during [[Act 7.5 — Nine Months]]. Her absence affects several of the people closest to her, including [[Predator]], [[Red Wind]], [[Champion]], [[Enigma (OD-139)]], [[Azure Gale]], and [[Bulwark]].

Life in Hell provides a rare period of relative normality before the Hell Hosts once again become involved in larger events across the galaxy.

# Sub-Acts

## 11.5.1 — Home

The opening story follows an ordinary day on [[Voridinus II]].

Rather than focusing on a single character, the story moves throughout the Hell Host stronghold, showing different levels of the organization functioning simultaneously.

High Command coordinates military operations.

Doom Corps personnel train and prepare for deployment.

Aircraft move between military installations.

Soldiers eat, sleep, argue, work, and socialise.

Robotic personnel move alongside biological soldiers as an ordinary part of military life.

Characters such as [[Predator]], [[Azure Gale]], [[Champion]], [[Bulwark]], [[Marksman]], [[Red Wind]], and [[Enigma (OD-139)]] provide different perspectives on the faction they call home.

Despite the normality, [[Destiny]] remains absent.

The Hell Hosts continue without her.

The people closest to her do not.

---

## 11.5.2 — We Are the Wall

This story focuses on [[Bulwark]] and his responsibilities as commander of the [[Doom Corps]].

Rather than depicting Bulwark through a major battle, the story follows the quieter responsibilities that have earned him the respect of the soldiers under his command.

Inspections, casualty reports, wounded personnel, battalion commanders, memorials, training standards, and ordinary conversations demonstrate the enormous human responsibility behind commanding the Doom Corps.

[[Warden]] also appears as one of the senior officers working beneath Bulwark.

The story explores one of Bulwark's defining beliefs:

The Doom Corps is not simply an army.

It is made of people.

---

## 11.5.3 — The Vandrix Name

This story focuses on members of [[House Vandrix]] serving within the Hell Hosts.

Major characters include:

- [[Warden]]
- [[Deadeye]]
- [[Doom]]
- [[Striker]]
- [[Hope]]

Particular attention is given to [[Striker]], one of the younger members of the family.

Surrounded by relatives who already possess significant military reputations, Striker struggles with the fear that his own achievements will always be attributed to the Vandrix name.

The story explores military family expectations, sibling relationships, inherited reputation, and the difference between receiving a legacy and earning one.

---

## 11.5.4 — Wolves

This story focuses on the [[Wolf Unit]].

To most of the galaxy, [[Black Wolf]], [[White Wolf]], and [[Grey Wolf]] are terrifying hunters associated with disappearances, assassinations, and high-value target elimination.

Away from their missions, however, the three function less like a conventional military unit and more like a family.

The story explores the relationship between the Wolves without removing the mystery surrounding their operations.

[[Red Wind]] also takes an interest in the unit, particularly White Wolf.

---

## 11.5.5 — The Red Hand

This story focuses on the [[The Red Hand|Red Hand]] and its three members:

- [[Insurgent]]
- [[Hatchet]]
- [[Karma]]

Unlike the Wolves, the Red Hand is designed around escalation, suppression, and organizational collapse.

Its reputation creates an uncomfortable relationship with other Hell Host personnel, many of whom respect the unit's effectiveness without necessarily wanting to work beside it.

The story explores the people underneath that reputation and provides greater focus on Karma outside of combat.

---

## 11.5.6 — More Than Metal

This story explores the enormous robotic and synthetic population serving within the Hell Hosts.

Characters such as [[Enigma]], [[Viper (D3)|Viper]], [[B.O.B]], and [[Aeon Unit]] demonstrate the enormous differences that can exist between machine personnel.

Some machines were created specifically for warfare.

Others developed beyond their original purpose.

Some openly display personality and emotion.

Others insist that their decisions are entirely logical.

The story explores how biological and synthetic personnel coexist within the same military without treating every machine intelligence as identical.

---

## 11.5.7 — Fourth Fleet

This story focuses on [[Necromancer]] and the reputation surrounding [[Fourth Fleet]].

Necromancer remains one of the most unsettling officers within the Hell Hosts despite his continued loyalty to the faction.

Rather than revealing the truth behind his condition, the story explores how ordinary personnel perceive him.

Rumours follow him through military facilities.

Soldiers exchange contradictory stories about his abilities.

Some personnel avoid him entirely.

Others treat him simply as another commanding officer.

[[Ranger]] remains one of the few people apparently unaffected by Necromancer's reputation.

The story deliberately leaves the truth behind Necromancer unresolved.

---

## 11.5.8 — Leave

[[Ranger]] and [[Forsaken]] attempt to spend time away from active operations.

Their approaches are considerably different.

Forsaken prefers quiet streets, cafés, books, long walks, and places where nobody recognises him.

Ranger prefers drinking with soldiers, gambling, exploring settlements, competition, stories, and whatever questionable activity happens to attract his attention.

Their conflicting personalities turn what should have been an uneventful period of leave into something considerably less peaceful.

Despite their frequent disagreements, the story demonstrates the genuine respect between the two soldiers.

---

## 11.5.9 — Barracks

This story shifts attention away from famous commanders and toward everyday Doom Corps personnel.

Barracks life includes:

- Training
- Equipment maintenance
- Inspections
- Arguments
- Rumours
- Competitions
- Practical jokes
- Deployment preparation
- Complaints about officers
- Soldiers returning from operations
- New personnel adjusting to military life

Several younger and less experienced Hell Host characters appear throughout the story.

Rather than portraying the Hell Hosts entirely through legendary soldiers, Barracks explores what life is like for the personnel who form the overwhelming majority of the faction.

---

## 11.5.10 — Skyborne

This story focuses on the [[Aviation Division]] and the personnel responsible for keeping Hell Host forces in the air.

Pilots, mechanics, ground crews, rescue personnel, and aircraft crews operate within a culture distinct from conventional Doom Corps formations.

Characters such as [[Skyfall]] and [[Hardfall]] appear alongside the wider aviation personnel.

The story explores squadron culture, aircraft maintenance, pilot-mechanic relationships, inter-division rivalry, and the enormous logistical effort required to maintain Hell Host air power.

---

## 11.5.11 — Shadows

This story explores personnel whose duties frequently occur outside ordinary frontline warfare.

Characters may include:

- [[Umbra]]
- [[Hitman 0-7]]
- [[Living Shadow]]
- [[Whisper]]
- [[Haze]]

Military security, reconnaissance, covert operations, fugitive recovery, surveillance, and classified assignments all operate beneath the more visible side of the Hell Hosts.

The story explores the soldiers whose work is often unnoticed by the wider military despite its importance.

---

## 11.5.12 — After Hours

This story follows Hell Host personnel during their limited free time.

Cafeterias, recreational areas, bars, games, gambling, arguments, jokes, and conversations bring together characters who would rarely share the same battlefield assignment.

The story allows personnel from different divisions, battalions, fleets, and ranks to interact outside formal military structures.

[[Marksman]] proves particularly capable of causing problems.

[[Ranger]] finds opportunities for competition.

[[Enigma]] continues participating in human social rituals despite several of them being completely unnecessary for a machine.

The story also begins developing the relationship between [[Karma]] and [[Magnum]].

Their connection remains small and informal, establishing the beginning of a relationship rather than immediately defining it.

[[Champion]] also reflects upon his recent experiences working alongside [[Valkyrie]] during the temporary alliance with the [[Free World Order]].

---

## 11.5.13 — Red Wind

This story focuses entirely on [[Red Wind]] outside major military operations.

Despite being capable of commanding soldiers in battle, Red Wind continues to struggle with ordinary social interaction, isolation, and the absence of any memory of her life before the Hell Hosts.

Her quieter interests and routines are explored alongside her relationships with other personnel.

[[Ranger]] continues attempting to include her in social situations.

[[Bulwark]] provides one of the few environments where Red Wind feels little pressure to perform or explain herself.

The disappearance of [[Destiny]], one of Red Wind's closest friends, also continues to affect her.

The story provides Red Wind with something rarely available during war:

Time to think.

---

## 11.5.14 — Blood

This story focuses on [[Blood]] before the events of [[Act 15 — Blood's Ascension]].

Rather than focusing upon the power Blood will later acquire, the story establishes who Blood is beforehand.

Their routines, relationships, friendships, military role, and place within the Hell Hosts are given greater attention.

The story deliberately avoids treating Blood's future Ascension as inevitable.

At this point in their life, Blood is still simply a member of the Hell Hosts.

---

## 11.5.15 — Calypso

The final story focuses on [[Calypso]] before the events that will eventually lead to [[Act 18 — Calypso's Ascension]].

Like Blood, Calypso is explored as a person rather than through the abilities and consequences that will later define part of their story.

The story serves as a quiet conclusion to Life in Hell.

Across [[Voridinus II]], soldiers return to barracks.

Night shifts replace day shifts.

Aircraft continue moving between installations.

Fleets enter and leave orbit.

Machines continue working.

Commanders finish reports that will be replaced by new ones the following morning.

For once, there is no enormous battle deciding the future of the galaxy.

Life simply continues.

# Lore Introduced

The Act significantly expands everyday life within the [[Hell Hosts]], including:

- Military family culture
- Barracks life
- Off-duty activities
- Relationships between ranks
- Doom Corps traditions
- Aviation culture
- Robotic and synthetic military life
- Military leave
- Memorial culture
- High Command routines
- Inter-unit relationships
- Life on [[Voridinus II]] outside major battles

The Act also establishes a clearer personal baseline for several characters before later events dramatically change their lives.

---

# Consequences

Although Life in Hell contains no single galaxy-changing event, its character developments influence several later stories.

The Act strengthens relationships between Hell Host personnel and establishes parts of their lives that continue into later conflicts.

Most notably, it provides greater context for:

- [[Act 12 — Expansion into Prioxis]]
- [[Act 14 — Rescue of Destiny]]
- [[Act 15 — Blood's Ascension]]
- [[Act 17 — Cabin Gathering]]
- [[Act 18 — Calypso's Ascension]]

By focusing on the people living inside the Hell Hosts rather than the wars fought by them, Life in Hell establishes what those characters actually have to lose when the larger conflicts of [[Power Unbound]] begin escalating again.
