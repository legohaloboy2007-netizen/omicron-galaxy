# Elena Ward

**Full Name:** Elena Ward  
**Known As:** Chief Inspector Ward  
**Species:** [[Humans|Human]]  
**Gender:** Female  
**Age:** 39  
**Faction:** [[Protection and Crime Enforcement]]  
**Rank:** Chief Inspector  
**Assignment:** Tactical Response Division  
**Position:** Tactical Response Commander  
**Specialisation:** Hostage Rescue, Armed Response, High-Risk Arrests, Siege Resolution  
**Status:** Active  

**Elena Ward** is a Chief Inspector within [[Protection and Crime Enforcement]] and an experienced commander within the organisation's Tactical Response Division.

Responsible for responding to incidents considered too dangerous for conventional officers, Ward has spent much of her career dealing with armed suspects, hostage situations, fortified locations, terrorist incidents, violent organised crime, and other high-risk emergencies across populated worlds.

She has developed a reputation for remaining remarkably calm during situations in which seconds can determine whether officers, suspects, or civilians survive.

Ward does not judge the success of an operation by how quickly her team entered a building.

She judges it by how many people came back out.

## Appearance

Ward is a physically fit human woman of average height with an athletic build maintained through years of tactical service.

She has dark brown hair, normally tied back or kept short enough to remain practical beneath tactical equipment, and grey-green eyes.

Her appearance outside active operations is relatively unremarkable.

During tactical deployments, Ward wears specialised PCE Tactical Response armour designed primarily for ballistic protection, mobility, communications, and environmental adaptability.

Unlike military armour used by factions such as [[Hell Hosts]], PCE tactical equipment is visibly marked with law-enforcement identification.

Ward has no interest in making her officers look like anonymous soldiers.

They are police officers.

She believes civilians should be able to recognise that immediately.

## Personality

Ward is calm, observant, disciplined, and extremely difficult to provoke.

During active incidents, she speaks clearly and rarely raises her voice.

She dislikes officers who confuse aggression with confidence.

Outside operations, however, Ward is considerably warmer than her professional reputation initially suggests.

She knows the officers under her command personally, remembers details about their lives, and makes an effort to remain approachable despite her rank.

This is not accidental.

Ward believes tactical personnel who are uncomfortable speaking honestly with their commander become dangerous.

An officer who is exhausted needs to say they are exhausted.

An officer who does not understand an instruction needs to ask.

An officer who believes Ward has made the wrong decision is expected to tell her.

Once an operation begins, hesitation can kill.

Before it begins, disagreement is welcome.

## Career

Ward began her PCE career as an ordinary [[Protection and Crime Enforcement|PCE]] Officer.

Her earliest assignments involved conventional patrol work, responding to civilian emergencies, making arrests, assisting investigations, and dealing with the countless mundane incidents that occupy most law-enforcement personnel.

She later applied for tactical-response training.

Ward initially served as a Tactical Officer and participated in high-risk arrests and armed-response operations before gradually taking greater leadership responsibilities.

She eventually became a tactical team leader and later rose to the rank of Chief Inspector.

Her current position places her in command of major Tactical Response operations and allows her to coordinate multiple tactical elements during particularly dangerous incidents.

Despite her specialist career, Ward considers her years as a patrol officer some of the most important experience she possesses.

Tactical Response may arrive when an incident becomes extraordinary.

The officers already standing outside normally arrived when it was still ordinary.

Ward listens to them.

## Tactical Response Division

The Tactical Response Division is one of PCE's specialist armed-response organisations.

Its personnel are deployed when conventional officers encounter threats requiring equipment, training, or capabilities beyond normal patrol duties.

Common deployments include:

- Hostage situations
- Armed sieges
- High-risk warrants and arrests
- Terrorist incidents
- Fortified criminal positions
- Dangerous synthetic or cybernetic suspects
- Organised crime operations
- Active mass-casualty threats
- Situations involving military-grade weapons
- Certain anomalous incidents before specialist organisations assume control

Ward commands Tactical Response personnel during major operations.

Her responsibilities can include establishing containment, coordinating evacuation, approving entry plans, working with negotiators, directing specialist teams, communicating with senior PCE leadership, and ultimately deciding when tactical intervention becomes necessary.

## Command Philosophy

Ward believes the purpose of Tactical Response is not to win gunfights.

It is to prevent situations from becoming gunfights whenever possible and to end them decisively when prevention fails.

She considers an operation in which a suspect peacefully surrenders just as successful as one involving an exceptionally executed tactical assault.

Usually more successful.

Ward therefore makes extensive use of containment, negotiation, surveillance, remote systems, intelligence gathering, and patience.

She is willing to wait hours for an advantage if civilians are not in immediate danger.

However, her preference for controlled resolution should not be mistaken for reluctance to use force.

When Ward determines that someone presents an immediate threat to civilian or officer life, she acts quickly.

Once an assault has been authorised, her command style becomes extremely direct.

Every officer has an objective.

Every entrance has a purpose.

Every weapon has a reason to be there.

## Use of Force

Ward is neither opposed to lethal force nor comfortable with unnecessary violence.

She considers both attitudes dangerous.

Tactical officers under her command are expected to understand the difference between someone who is dangerous and someone who must immediately be killed.

Whenever circumstances permit, suspects are arrested.

Ward believes this is important for reasons beyond morality.

Living suspects can be questioned.

Evidence can be recovered.

Networks can be identified.

Victims can receive answers.

Courts can determine guilt.

Killing everyone inside a building may sometimes be easier.

It is rarely better police work.

## Relationship with Commissioner Thompson

Ward has worked under [[Gareth Thompson|Commissioner Gareth Thompson]] during numerous major incidents.

Their approaches to law enforcement differ considerably.

Thompson is willing to authorise extreme measures when he believes they are necessary to preserve order.

Ward is often the person responsible for determining whether those measures are actually necessary.

This has occasionally resulted in disagreements.

Ward does not openly challenge senior authority for the sake of demonstrating independence, nor does Thompson expect blind agreement from experienced commanders.

If Ward believes an operation is unnecessarily dangerous, she will say so.

If Thompson orders an objective achieved, Ward will attempt to achieve it using the safest viable method available.

Their professional relationship is therefore built less upon personal similarity than mutual trust in each other's responsibilities.

Thompson decides what must be accomplished.

Ward determines how her people will accomplish it.

Neither always likes the other's answer.

## Leadership

Ward is fiercely protective of personnel under her command without becoming unwilling to place them in danger.

She understands that tactical policing is inherently dangerous.

Her responsibility is not to eliminate risk.

It is to ensure that nobody takes a risk without a reason.

Ward therefore has little tolerance for officers who unnecessarily expose themselves to danger, abandon formation, pursue fleeing suspects alone, or attempt heroic actions without communicating with their team.

She is particularly hostile toward the idea that dying in the line of duty somehow proves dedication.

Ward would rather explain why an operation took twenty minutes longer than explain to someone's family why an officer never came home.

Her personnel know this.

Many trust her because of it.

## Equipment

During tactical operations, Ward uses standardised PCE Tactical Response equipment rather than highly personalised weaponry.

Her equipment typically includes:

- PCE tactical body armour
- Enclosed or semi-enclosed tactical helmet
- Integrated communications system
- Low-light and thermal optics
- Respiratory protection
- Tactical carbine
- P2-series sidearm
- Restraints
- Medical supplies
- Breaching and detection equipment as required

Additional equipment is selected according to the incident.

Ward dislikes carrying equipment simply because it looks impressive.

If an officer cannot explain why they need something for an operation, Ward generally does not want them carrying it.

## Combat

Ward has no anomalous abilities, biological enhancements, or superhuman powers.

Her effectiveness comes from training and experience.

She is proficient with firearms, close-quarters combat, room clearing, breaching procedures, tactical movement, and incident command.

However, Ward's greatest strength is her ability to process rapidly changing situations while maintaining control of the personnel around her.

She does not attempt to personally outperform younger Tactical Officers.

Her job is to ensure they are standing in the right place, looking in the right direction, with the right information when something happens.

Ward can fight.

More importantly, she knows when not to.

## Civilian Protection

Civilian safety is one of Ward's highest operational priorities.

She is particularly cautious when conducting operations in densely populated environments where missed shots, structural damage, fires, explosive decompression, damaged infrastructure, or panicked crowds can cause casualties far beyond the original incident.

This becomes especially important aboard spacecraft and orbital stations.

A weapon capable of stopping an armoured suspect may also be capable of penetrating the wall behind them.

Ward expects her personnel to remember both facts.

## Aftermath

Ward considers an operation unfinished when the shooting stops.

Following major incidents, she personally reviews tactical decisions, officer actions, equipment performance, injuries, intelligence failures, and unexpected developments.

She is known for extensive operational debriefings.

Mistakes are examined openly.

Ward does not expect perfection.

She expects mistakes to become lessons rather than traditions.

She also takes the psychological effects of tactical work seriously.

Officers involved in particularly traumatic incidents may be temporarily removed from active tactical duty regardless of whether they insist they are capable of continuing.

Ward has received plenty of complaints about this policy.

She has never changed it.

## Reputation

Within PCE, Ward is regarded as a highly capable tactical commander with an unusually controlled approach to armed response.

Some officers consider her excessively cautious.

Others consider her the exact person they want commanding them when an operation goes wrong.

Criminal organisations operating within her jurisdiction have learned that Ward's preference for peaceful resolution should never be interpreted as weakness.

She will offer an opportunity to surrender.

She will explain exactly what happens if that opportunity is refused.

And if the situation leaves her no other reasonable option, she will send the team through the door.
