# Fire Division

> [!info] Division Profile  
> **Faction:** [[Hell Hosts]]  
> **Type:** Artillery / Siege Warfare Division  
> **Status:** Active  
> **Primary Role:** Heavy fire support, artillery warfare, siege operations  
> **Secondary Roles:** Counter-battery fire, rocket bombardment, defensive emplacements, smoke deployment, orbital fire coordination  
> **Origins:** Former [[Free Order Rebellion]] and [[Terrorism Control Agency]] personnel  
> **Motto:** _“Rain Hell Upon Our Enemies.”_  
> **Known For:** Extreme long-range firepower, veteran artillery culture, former FOR/TCA integration, and overwhelming preparatory bombardment  
> **Primary Systems:** [[R96 Rotary Artillery Cannon]], [[445 Tributary Artillery Cannon]]

---

## Overview

The **Fire Division** is the primary artillery and heavy fire-support branch of the [[Hell Hosts]] military.

Unlike the [[Doom Corps]], which forms the backbone of conventional ground warfare, the Fire Division specialises in destroying enemy positions from extreme range before friendly infantry ever reaches them.

Its responsibilities include:

- Long-range artillery
    
- Siege warfare
    
- Rocket bombardment
    
- Defensive fire support
    
- Counter-battery operations
    
- Fortification destruction
    
- Battlefield suppression
    
- Smoke and obscuration
    
- Orbital fire coordination
    
- Heavy anti-armour bombardment
    

The division traces its origins directly back to the [[TCA-FOR War]].

Its earliest members were not comrades.

They were enemies.

Today, that history has become one of the defining parts of Fire Division culture.

Their purpose is simple:

> **Find the target.**

> **Calculate the solution.**

> **Rain Hell Upon Our Enemies.**

---

# Origins

Before the formation of the Hell Hosts, the [[Free Order Rebellion]] and [[Terrorism Control Agency]] fought one another throughout the TCA-FOR War.

Both organisations fielded large conventional infantry forces.

When the conflict eventually ended and the Hell Hosts were established, enormous numbers of surviving FOR and TCA personnel entered the new military structure.

Many transferred into the newly developing:

[[Doom Corps]]

However, a significant number remained within their original formations.

Hell Host Command faced a problem.

The faction did not need two additional conventional infantry branches.

It already had Doom Corps.

Rather than disbanding thousands of experienced soldiers, the remaining FOR and TCA formations were reorganised into a new specialised branch.

That branch became:

> **The Fire Division.**

---

# From Infantry to Artillery

The transition was significant.

Former riflemen became:

- Artillery crews
    
- Siege specialists
    
- Forward observers
    
- Fire-control personnel
    
- Rocket battery operators
    
- Heavy weapon technicians
    
- Target coordinators
    

Old infantry commanders were retrained in:

- Artillery doctrine
    
- Long-range targeting
    
- Siege warfare
    
- Battlefield logistics
    
- Fire-support coordination
    

The division gradually abandoned its role as a conventional infantry force.

In its place emerged one of the most destructive artillery organisations in the Omicron Galaxy.

---

# Former Enemies

The early Fire Division was not particularly comfortable.

Former FOR soldiers were suddenly expected to work beside former TCA personnel they may have fought only months or years earlier.

Old rivalries survived.

Arguments were common.

Some crews initially refused to work together.

Others spent years insulting one another over battles that had already ended.

Eventually, repeated deployments changed the culture.

A gun crew did not survive because everyone agreed politically.

It survived because:

- The loader worked
    
- The gunner worked
    
- The spotter worked
    
- The targeting data was correct
    
- The round landed where it was supposed to
    

Over time, the previous uniforms mattered less.

The artillery did not care who had once worn which insignia.

---

## Old Fire Division Saying

> **“Different uniforms. Same gun line.”**

The phrase originated during the division's early integration period.

It remains popular among veteran personnel.

---

# Division Doctrine

Fire Division doctrine is based around one principle:

> **Destroy the enemy before the enemy reaches you.**

Where Doom Corps doctrine frequently focuses on taking and holding ground, Fire Division doctrine focuses on shaping that ground before the infantry arrives.

Typical objectives include:

- Destroying defensive positions
    
- Suppressing enemy movement
    
- Breaking fortified lines
    
- Eliminating artillery batteries
    
- Disabling armour concentrations
    
- Cutting retreat routes
    
- Destroying supply columns
    
- Providing smoke cover
    
- Preventing reinforcement
    
- Preparing landing zones
    

A Fire Division strike may begin long before the enemy realises a ground assault is coming.

---

# Fire Support Doctrine

Hell Host artillery rarely fires without purpose.

Fire missions are generally classified according to role.

---

## Suppression Fire

Used to keep enemy personnel under cover while Hell Host forces move.

Common targets include:

- Trenches
    
- Rooftops
    
- Defensive walls
    
- Infantry positions
    
- Anti-aircraft crews
    

---

## Destruction Fire

Designed to physically destroy a target.

Typical targets include:

- Fortifications
    
- Vehicles
    
- Artillery
    
- Command posts
    
- Bunkers
    
- Bridges
    

---

## Interdiction Fire

Used to prevent enemy movement.

This may involve striking:

- Roads
    
- Rail lines
    
- Convoys
    
- Reinforcement corridors
    
- Extraction routes
    

---

## Counter-Battery Fire

Used specifically against enemy artillery.

Fire Division counter-battery crews specialise in detecting hostile firing positions and returning fire before the opposing battery can relocate.

---

## Preparatory Bombardment

Conducted immediately before a major Hell Host assault.

The objective is to:

- Destroy fortifications
    
- Damage enemy morale
    
- Force defenders underground
    
- Disable communications
    
- Clear assault corridors
    

Preparatory bombardment is closely coordinated with the [[Doom Corps]].

---

# The Forward Observers

Fire Division artillery may operate dozens of kilometres from the battlefield.

Someone still has to see the target.

That responsibility belongs to:

> **Forward Observers**

Fire Division observers frequently deploy alongside Doom Corps personnel.

Their role includes:

- Target identification
    
- Range calculation
    
- Fire correction
    
- Damage assessment
    
- Artillery coordination
    
- Emergency fire requests
    

Forward observers may operate dangerously close to enemy formations.

A single observer can direct enough artillery to change the outcome of an entire battle.

Because of this, experienced observers are highly respected within the Fire Division.

---

# Fire Division Structure

The Fire Division uses a mixture of battalion-scale and battery-scale formations.

The basic artillery structure generally follows:

> **Gun Crew → Battery → Artillery Group → Battalion**

---

## Gun Crew

The smallest operational artillery element.

A gun crew is responsible for a single artillery platform or specialised weapons system.

Typical responsibilities include:

- Loading
    
- Firing
    
- Maintenance
    
- Targeting
    
- Ammunition handling
    

---

## Battery

A Battery contains multiple artillery pieces operating together.

Batteries may specialise in:

- RAC systems
    
- TAC systems
    
- Rocket artillery
    
- Heavy mortars
    
- Counter-battery warfare
    
- Siege operations
    

---

## Artillery Group

Multiple Batteries may be combined into a larger Artillery Group for planetary campaigns.

Groups are frequently assigned to support specific Doom Corps formations.

---

## Battalion

Battalions form the largest permanent combat identities within the Fire Division.

Some specialise heavily in a particular form of artillery warfare.

# R96 Rotary Artillery Cannon

> [!danger] Primary Artillery System  
> **Designation:** R96 RAC  
> **Full Name:** R96 Rotary Artillery Cannon  
> **Role:** Sustained bombardment / area suppression  
> **Barrels:** Four rotating artillery barrels  
> **Speciality:** High-volume fire

The [[R96 Rotary Artillery Cannon]] is one of the Fire Division's signature weapons.

Unlike conventional artillery pieces that focus primarily on individual shell power, the RAC is designed around:

> **Volume.**

Its four rotating barrels allow it to sustain unusually rapid artillery fire across a large area.

The system is frequently used for:

- Area denial
    
- Infantry suppression
    
- Defensive saturation
    
- Anti-fortification fire
    
- Preparing assault zones
    

Individual shells may be less destructive than those fired by the 445 TAC.

The RAC compensates by firing considerably more of them.

---

## RAC Reputation

Doom Corps soldiers sometimes describe RAC bombardment as:

> **“Watching an entire grid square get chewed apart.”**

The description is not considered technically accurate.

Fire Division personnel rarely object.

---

# 445 Tributary Artillery Cannon

> [!danger] Heavy Siege System  
> **Designation:** 445 TAC  
> **Full Name:** 445 Tributary Artillery Cannon  
> **Role:** Heavy siege warfare  
> **Speciality:** Maximum destructive firepower

The [[445 Tributary Artillery Cannon]] is the heavier counterpart to the R96 RAC.

The TAC trades:

- Rate of fire
    
- Range
    
- Deployment speed
    

for significantly greater destructive power.

It is designed primarily for:

- Fortress destruction
    
- Anti-bunker warfare
    
- Heavy siege operations
    
- Defensive structure elimination
    
- Hardened target destruction
    

A single TAC shell can cause catastrophic damage to fortified targets.

---

# TAC Mobility

The TAC uses an advanced mobile propulsion system allowing it to cross terrain that would immobilise conventional artillery.

Depending on model and configuration, this may include assisted hover-track mobility across:

- Rivers
    
- Marshlands
    
- Shallow water
    
- Mud
    
- Broken terrain
    

This allows TAC batteries to reposition without relying entirely on prepared roads.

Fire Division crews are known for developing unusually close relationships with their artillery pieces.

Many guns receive names.

These names may be:

- Serious
    
- Historical
    
- Sentimental
    
- Completely ridiculous
    

A gun's official designation may be:

> **R96-48117**

Its crew may insist the weapon is actually called:

> **Divorce Settlement**

No explanation is normally provided.

# Crew Culture

Fire Division culture is heavily influenced by:

- Former FOR personnel
    
- Former TCA personnel
    
- Veteran artillery crews
    
- Long deployments
    
- Dark humour
    

The division tends to possess less ceremonial behaviour than many other Hell Host branches.

Crew relationships are often informal.

Ammunition loaders may openly argue with officers.

Veteran gunners regularly insult newly commissioned personnel.

Everyone stops arguing when coordinates arrive.

---

# Accuracy Culture

Fire Division personnel take accuracy extremely seriously.

Missing a target is not simply considered wasted ammunition.

A missed artillery shell may:

- Reveal the battery
    
- Endanger allies
    
- Allow enemy forces to escape
    
- Destroy civilian infrastructure
    
- Waste valuable ammunition
    

As a result, Fire Division training heavily emphasises:

- Target confirmation
    
- Ballistic calculation
    
- Atmospheric conditions
    
- Planetary gravity
    
- Terrain effects
    
- Friendly-force positioning
    

The fact that Hell Host operations occur across different planets makes artillery calculation significantly more complicated than traditional warfare.

---

# Planetary Artillery

Gravity is not identical across the Omicron Galaxy.

Atmospheres differ.

Weather systems differ.

Air pressure differs.

A firing solution effective on one world may be completely wrong on another.

Fire Division crews therefore maintain extensive planetary artillery data.

Modern targeting computers perform much of this calculation automatically.

Experienced crews are still trained to understand it manually.

This ensures artillery remains operational even during:

- Electronic warfare
    
- Sensor disruption
    
- Communication failure
    

---

# Relationship with Doom Corps

The [[Doom Corps]] maintains one of the closest operational relationships with Fire Division.

Doom Corps captures ground.

Fire Division shapes that ground beforehand.

Typical joint operations involve:

1. Reconnaissance identifies the enemy position.
    
2. Forward observers confirm targets.
    
3. Fire Division begins bombardment.
    
4. Enemy defensive capability is degraded.
    
5. Doom Corps advances.
    
6. Fire Division shifts fire ahead of the assault.
    

The two divisions maintain strong mutual respect.

Doom Corps soldiers frequently know supporting artillery batteries by name.

---

## Doom Corps Saying

> **“If Fire says keep your head down, keep your head down.”**

---

# Relationship with Aviation Division

The [[Aviation Division]] and Fire Division cooperate extensively during planetary operations.

Typical coordination includes:

- Air reconnaissance
    
- Target spotting
    
- Suppression
    
- Smoke corridors
    
- Counter-air operations
    
- Combined bombardment
    

The two divisions also maintain a long-running rivalry.

Aviation personnel occasionally claim aircraft can destroy targets faster than artillery.

Fire Division personnel typically respond:

> **“Then why didn't you?”**

---

# Relationship with Eradication Division

Combined Fire and [[Eradication Division]] deployments are considered a serious escalation.

Fire Division frequently performs the initial bombardment.

Eradication enters afterward.

Typical sequence:

> **Fire destroys the position.**

> **Eradication destroys what survived.**

These combined operations are rarely authorised near civilian areas.

The battalion specialises in:

## Relationship with Doom

[[Doom]], real name **Alexia Vandrix**, is heavily associated with the Iron Casters and the battalion's aggressive siege doctrine.

Under her leadership, the unit became increasingly integrated with direct assault operations.

Rather than remaining entirely behind friendly lines, the Iron Casters became known for advancing artillery support close behind Doom Corps assault formations.

Their philosophy became:

> **Break the wall.**

> **Then break whatever was hiding behind it.**

# Operation: Playing with Fire

> [!success] Major Fire Division Victory  
> **Enemy:** [[Angel Sword]]  
> **Primary Force:** [[51st Long-Range Artillery Battalion]]  
> **Engagement Range:** Approximately 50 km  
> **Duration:** Approximately five minutes  
> **Result:** Complete destruction of hostile convoy

[[Operation Playing with Fire]] is one of the most famous Fire Division engagements in Hell Host history.

An Angel Sword convoy was detected travelling approximately:

**50 kilometres**

from a Hell Host artillery position.

The convoy consisted of military transports, supply vehicles, armoured elements, and supporting personnel.

Hell Host Command determined that the force had to be destroyed before it reached its destination.

Fire Division received the coordinates.

No Doom Corps interception force was deployed.

No Aviation strike was required.

The artillery crews handled it themselves.

---

## Opening Barrage

The 51st Hellrainers calculated the firing solution and began bombardment.

The first shells struck the convoy before Angel Sword personnel had fully identified the source of the attack.

The convoy attempted to:

- Scatter
    
- Accelerate
    
- Seek cover
    
- Return fire
    
- Change route
    

Fire Division observers continuously updated the firing solution.

Follow-up barrages shifted with the convoy.

Vehicles were destroyed faster than survivors could reorganise.

---

## Five Minutes

The entire engagement lasted approximately:

**Five minutes.**

By the end of the operation, the Angel Sword convoy had been completely destroyed.

One version of the after-action summary records:

> **OPERATION DURATION:** 00:05:17  
> **HELL HOST CASUALTIES:** 0  
> **HOSTILE CONVOY:** DESTROYED

The exact timing has become legendary within Fire Division culture.

Some newly assigned personnel initially assume the story is exaggerated.

Veterans are happy to correct them.

---

# Legacy of Playing with Fire

Operation Playing with Fire became one of the clearest demonstrations of what long-range Hell Host artillery could accomplish.

The enemy never reached visual range.

The enemy never fired upon the Hell Host artillery line.

The enemy never got close enough to matter.

The operation became strongly associated with the Fire Division motto:

> **“Rain Hell Upon Our Enemies.”**

---

# Fire Division and Defeat

The Fire Division does not maintain a culture of pretending every bombardment succeeds.

Artillery is vulnerable to:

- Counter-battery attacks
    
- Air strikes
    
- Electronic warfare
    
- Ammunition shortages
    
- Poor intelligence
    
- Rapid enemy movement
    
- Detection
    
- Sabotage
    

Several disastrous Fire Division operations exist within Hell Host history.

Some remain poorly documented.

Others are still being recovered from older military archives.

The division studies its failures with the same seriousness as its victories.

> **A gun line that believes it cannot be destroyed is already dead.**

# Traditions

## Gun Naming

Artillery pieces are frequently given unofficial names by their crews.

A weapon may retain the same name through:

- Repairs
    
- Upgrades
    
- Crew changes
    
- Multiple campaigns
    

Retired guns occasionally have their name plates preserved at Fire Division bases.

---

## First Round

New artillery crews traditionally preserve the casing, ignition component, or firing record from their first live combat mission.

Some crews engrave:

- Date
    
- World
    
- Target
    
- Crew names
    

into the preserved component.

---

## Final Round

When an artillery piece is permanently retired, its final combat firing is recorded.

The firing record may later be displayed alongside the weapon's operational history.

---

## Old Uniform Tradition

Some veteran Fire Division personnel preserve small fragments of former FOR or TCA insignia inside:

- Lockers
    
- Gun compartments
    
- Personal equipment
    

The symbols are not worn publicly as active allegiance.

They represent where the soldier came from.

The Hell Host insignia represents where they ended up.

---

# Motto

The official Fire Division motto is:

> **“Rain Hell Upon Our Enemies.”**

It represents both the division's artillery role and its place within the Hell Hosts.

The phrase is frequently:

- Painted onto artillery
    
- Displayed inside battery command centres
    
- Used before major fire missions

Fire Division equipment typically uses traditional Hell Host military colours combined with artillery-specific identification markings.

Common features include:

- Matte black armour
    
- Gunmetal equipment

Competitive.

## Among Enemy Forces

Fire Division is feared because visual contact is unnecessary.

- Detected
    
- Targeted
    
- Fired upon

# Division Identity

The Fire Division is built from contradictions.

Its founders were enemies.

Its crews fight from distance.

Its effects are felt up close.

It is one of the oldest reminders that the Hell Hosts were not created from a single army.

They were created from survivors.

Former FOR.

Former TCA.

New recruits.

Old enemies.

All standing behind the same guns.

The uniforms changed.

The targets changed.

The artillery remained.

And when the order is given:

> **They rain Hell upon their enemies.**
