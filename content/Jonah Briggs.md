# Jonah Briggs

**Full Name:** Jonah Briggs  
**Known As:** Sergeant Briggs  
**Species:** [[Humans|Human]]  
**Gender:** Male  
**Age:** 35  
**Faction:** [[Protection and Crime Enforcement]]  
**Rank:** Sergeant  
**Assignment:** Tactical Response Division  
**Position:** Tactical Response Team Leader  
**Specialisation:** Close-Quarters Operations, Breaching, High-Risk Arrests  
**Status:** Active  

**Jonah Briggs** is a Sergeant within the [[Protection and Crime Enforcement]] Tactical Response Division and serves as a frontline Tactical Response Team Leader under [[Elena Ward|Chief Inspector Elena Ward]].

Where Ward coordinates the wider tactical operation, Briggs is responsible for the officers physically carrying it out.

He leads entry teams during high-risk arrests, hostage rescues, armed sieges, and other incidents requiring direct intervention.

Briggs has spent years operating within confined environments where a bad decision can kill an officer, civilian, suspect, or all three.

He consequently has very little interest in heroics.

## Appearance

Briggs is a heavily built human male standing approximately 6'1".

He has short dark-blond hair, blue-grey eyes, and a weathered appearance resulting from years of operational service.

His nose has been broken at least once and healed slightly crooked.

Briggs generally maintains short facial stubble when regulations permit it.

During deployments, he wears heavy PCE Tactical Response armour with clear law-enforcement markings.

His equipment shows considerably more wear than Ward's command gear, particularly around the knees, forearms, and protective plating.

Briggs uses his equipment.

He does not decorate it.

## Personality

Briggs is direct, practical, dry-humoured, and extremely dependable.

He has little patience for unnecessary complexity.

If a plan requires fifteen minutes to explain, Briggs generally believes somebody has overthought it.

This does not make him unintelligent.

Briggs is an experienced tactical operator capable of rapidly understanding complicated environments and adapting plans when circumstances change.

He simply believes complexity creates opportunities for mistakes.

His communication during operations is therefore deliberately concise.

Outside tactical situations, Briggs is noticeably more relaxed.

He frequently jokes with other Tactical Response personnel and has developed a reputation for particularly terrible humour during long periods of waiting.

Ward suspects this is partly how he manages stress.

Briggs has never confirmed it.

## Career

Briggs began his PCE career as a conventional Officer before transferring into Tactical Response.

Unlike [[Elena Ward]], whose career gradually moved toward large-scale incident command, Briggs remained strongly attached to frontline tactical work.

He progressed from Tactical Officer to Senior Officer and eventually Sergeant.

His promotion placed him in command of his own Tactical Response team.

Briggs has participated in high-risk arrests, armed sieges, hostage incidents, raids against organised criminal groups, counter-terror operations, and responses involving suspects equipped with military-grade weapons.

He has been offered opportunities to move further away from frontline operations.

So far, he has declined.

Briggs likes knowing exactly what his officers are being asked to do.

Standing beside them makes that considerably easier.

## Tactical Response Team Leader

As a Tactical Response Team Leader, Briggs commands a small group of specialist officers during direct tactical operations.

His responsibilities include:

- Leading entry teams
- Assigning positions and responsibilities
- Conducting final equipment checks
- Coordinating breaching procedures
- Maintaining communication with tactical command
- Adapting entry plans as conditions change
- Coordinating civilian and hostage extraction
- Directing arrests
- Managing immediate threats
- Ensuring personnel accountability during withdrawal

During larger incidents, Briggs operates beneath the command of Chief Inspector Ward.

Ward controls the operation.

Briggs controls the room.

The distinction is important.

## Command Style

Briggs does not expect his officers to follow him blindly.

He expects them to understand the plan.

Before an operation begins, every member of his team should know their objective, route, responsibilities, fallback position, and what circumstances would require the plan to change.

Once the operation begins, communication becomes considerably shorter.

Briggs dislikes unnecessary radio chatter.

Information should be relevant.

Instructions should be clear.

Nobody needs a speech while standing outside a door containing an armed suspect.

## The Stack

Briggs frequently positions himself near the front of an entry formation.

This is partially tactical.

It is also personal.

He is uncomfortable ordering younger Tactical Officers into an environment he would refuse to enter himself.

Ward has occasionally reminded him that a Sergeant's responsibility is to lead the team rather than personally absorb every bullet intended for it.

Briggs agrees with this assessment.

His behaviour has changed only slightly.

## Breaching

Briggs specialises in forced entry and close-quarters tactical operations.

He is trained in mechanical, ballistic, explosive, and technological breaching methods.

However, he strongly prefers the least destructive method capable of accomplishing the objective.

Doors can conceal civilians.

Walls can contain infrastructure.

Explosive decompression aboard spacecraft remains universally inconvenient.

Briggs therefore treats breaching as a technical discipline rather than an excuse to destroy architecture.

He is nevertheless exceptionally good at destroying architecture when circumstances require it.

## Combat

Briggs possesses extensive training in close-quarters combat, firearms, tactical movement, breaching, and team-based engagements.

He has no anomalous abilities or biological enhancements.

His effectiveness depends upon training, equipment, communication, and experience.

Briggs is particularly dangerous in confined environments where coordinated movement and rapid threat identification are more valuable than raw physical ability.

He does not attempt to fight fairly.

Neither does he attempt to fight spectacularly.

He attempts to end dangerous situations before they become worse.

## Use of Force

Briggs shares Ward's belief that Tactical Response exists to resolve incidents rather than generate casualties.

He will use lethal force when necessary.

He will also happily arrest someone without firing a shot.

Briggs has participated in operations where suspects surrendered immediately upon seeing Tactical Response enter.

He considers these among his favourite operations.

Less paperwork.

## Equipment

Briggs uses heavier equipment than conventional PCE patrol officers.

His standard tactical loadout commonly includes:

- Heavy PCE Tactical Response armour
- Enclosed ballistic helmet
- Integrated communications
- Thermal and low-light optics
- Respiratory protection
- Tactical carbine
- P2-series sidearm
- Less-lethal equipment
- Restraints
- Medical supplies
- Breaching tools

Depending upon the operation, Briggs may additionally carry a breaching shotgun, compact mechanical breacher, ballistic shield, or specialised explosive charges.

His loadout changes according to the mission.

Briggs strongly dislikes officers treating equipment selection as a competition to see who can carry the most weapons.

## Relationship with Elena Ward

Briggs has served under [[Elena Ward|Chief Inspector Elena Ward]] during numerous tactical operations.

Their relationship is built upon substantial professional trust.

Ward trusts Briggs to translate an operational plan into practical action.

Briggs trusts Ward not to send his team somewhere without understanding the consequences.

They occasionally disagree.

Briggs is considerably more willing than many Sergeants to tell Ward when he believes a plan will fail.

Ward specifically expects him to do so.

Their disagreements therefore tend to occur before operations rather than during them.

Once Ward and Briggs agree on a plan, both expect the other to follow through.

Briggs has described Ward's planning style as:

**"Annoyingly thorough."**

Ward considered this a compliment.

Briggs insists it was not.

## Relationship with Commissioner Thompson

Briggs has considerably less direct contact with [[Gareth Thompson|Commissioner Gareth Thompson]].

When Thompson becomes personally involved in an incident, Briggs generally assumes the situation has already become complicated.

He respects Thompson's experience but occasionally finds the Commissioner's willingness to authorise aggressive action uncomfortable.

Briggs does not object to dangerous orders simply because they are dangerous.

Danger is part of his profession.

He objects when danger accomplishes nothing.

If Thompson wants a building taken, Briggs wants to know why.

If the answer is good enough, he will take it.

## Relationship with Patrol Officers

Briggs treats conventional PCE Officers with considerably more respect than some specialist personnel expect.

He remembers being one.

Patrol officers frequently possess information unavailable to Tactical Response when they arrive.

They know which door the suspect entered.

They spoke with the witness.

They saw the weapon.

They know where civilians were last observed.

Briggs therefore makes a point of speaking with first responders before an assault whenever circumstances allow.

He has little patience for Tactical Officers who treat ordinary patrol personnel as amateurs.

Specialisation does not make someone universally competent.

It makes them specialised.

## Officer Safety

Briggs is intensely protective of the officers assigned to him.

This does not mean he avoids dangerous operations.

It means he refuses to accept preventable casualties.

After every deployment, Briggs personally confirms that every member of his team has returned.

This habit continues even when personnel accountability has already been electronically confirmed.

He wants to see them.

Ward has never asked him to stop.

## Injuries

Briggs has accumulated several injuries throughout his tactical career.

Most are relatively minor consequences of years spent performing physically demanding work.

He has been shot once while wearing armour and still experiences occasional discomfort around the impact site.

Briggs generally avoids discussing the incident.

According to him:

**"The armour worked. That's the interesting part."**

## Reputation

Within Tactical Response, Sergeant Briggs is regarded as an experienced and dependable team leader.

He is not particularly interested in promotion, public recognition, or building a reputation outside the Division.

His priorities are considerably smaller.

Complete the objective.

Protect civilians.

Bring the suspect in if possible.

Bring the team home.

Then complain about the paperwork.

Briggs has spent enough time standing outside dangerous doors to understand that eventually, one of them will open onto something nobody expected.

Until then, he checks his weapon, checks the officer beside him, waits for Ward's command—

and goes through first.
