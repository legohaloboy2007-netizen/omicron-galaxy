# Robotic Assault Machines Division

> *"If infantry cannot hold it, send something that doesn't bleed."*

The **Robotic Assault Machines Division**, commonly abbreviated to **R.A.M.**, is a heavy mechanized formation of the [[Hell Hosts]] built around large robotic combat platforms.

R.A.M. units are deployed wherever conventional infantry require sustained firepower, extreme durability, heavy lifting capability, or the ability to operate in environments considered excessively dangerous for organic personnel.

Unlike humanoid combat robots designed to function alongside ordinary infantry, Robotic Assault Machines are closer to **walking armored vehicles**.

They are large, heavily protected, and capable of carrying weapons normally impractical for individual soldiers.

Common armaments include heavy ballistic cannons, automatic weapons, missile systems, directed-energy weapons, and specialized equipment configured according to the machine's assigned role.

Despite the name, R.A.M. units are not exclusively offensive weapons.

They are used throughout Hell Host territory for frontline warfare, defensive operations, escort missions, logistical support, and the protection of strategically important locations.

---

## Role

R.A.M. formations are designed to provide heavy mechanized support without requiring conventional crewed vehicles for every task.

Their responsibilities include:

- Frontline assault
- Infantry support
- Defensive operations
- Outpost protection
- Installation security
- Convoy escort
- Cargo transportation
- Heavy weapons support
- Anti-vehicle combat
- Breaching fortified positions
- Hazardous-environment operations
- Battlefield recovery
- Perimeter defense

The machines are particularly valuable during prolonged engagements where fatigue, radiation, extreme temperatures, atmospheric hazards, or biological threats would place organic personnel at significant risk.

# Robotic Assault Machines

R.A.M. is both the name of the division and the general designation given to its principal combat platforms.

Individual machines vary enormously in size and configuration.

Smaller R.A.M.s may operate alongside infantry and armored formations, while heavier platforms can resemble mobile fortifications.

Most are constructed around a heavily armored central chassis supported by multiple mechanical limbs or reinforced tracked assemblies.

Their exact appearance depends upon role.

Unlike [[Androids]], R.A.M.s are not designed to resemble humans.

Unlike ordinary military robots, they are not primarily intended to replicate infantry.

They are machines designed around the assumption that there is no reason a robot needs to possess human proportions when it could instead carry several tonnes of armor and an autocannon.

---

# Artificial Intelligence

Not every R.A.M. possesses a fully sapient artificial intelligence.

Most combat platforms operate using advanced battlefield automation capable of:

- Target identification
- Navigation
- Formation coordination
- Threat prioritization
- Fire-control calculations
- Friendly-force recognition
- Terrain analysis
- Defensive positioning
- Following complex mission parameters

These systems allow R.A.M.s to operate independently for extended periods while remaining subordinate to Hell Host command structures.

More sophisticated platforms may possess considerably greater autonomy.

However, sapient artificial intelligences comparable to [[Enigma]] or [[Aeon Unit|Aegis]] are not required for ordinary R.A.M. operations.

A machine does not need a personality to understand:

**Hold this position.**

# Frontline Assault

R.A.M. units frequently advance alongside [[Doom Corps]] formations.

Their armor allows them to absorb fire that would force ordinary infantry into cover, while their heavier weapons can engage fortified positions, enemy vehicles, and concentrated infantry.

R.A.M.s are particularly effective when used as mobile anchors.

Infantry advance around them.

The machines suppress hostile positions.

Enemy anti-armor weapons are identified and eliminated.

Once the defensive line begins collapsing, conventional forces exploit the opening.

R.A.M.s are not intended to replace infantry.

They create conditions in which infantry can operate more effectively.

---

# Defensive Operations

One of the division's oldest responsibilities is the protection of Hell Host outposts and military installations.

R.A.M.s may be permanently stationed at:

- Forward operating bases
- Military compounds
- Supply depots
- Research facilities
- Spaceports
- Strategic infrastructure
- Planetary defense installations
- Remote observation posts

Their lack of fatigue makes them particularly useful for long-duration perimeter defense.

A R.A.M. can remain operational for extended periods without sleep, food, morale rotation, or many of the logistical requirements associated with organic guards.

This does not eliminate maintenance requirements.

It simply changes them.

---

# Convoy Escort

R.A.M.s are frequently assigned to high-value military convoys.

Their heavy armor and weapons allow them to act as mobile defensive platforms capable of responding rapidly to ambushes.

Depending upon configuration, R.A.M.s may travel alongside conventional vehicles or be physically integrated into cargo formations.

Escort machines are commonly tasked with:

- Detecting ambushes
- Engaging anti-vehicle teams
- Providing suppressive fire
- Clearing blocked routes
- Recovering disabled vehicles
- Protecting cargo
- Establishing emergency defensive positions

R.A.M.s are especially valuable when transporting supplies through territory where hostile forces possess significant anti-infantry capability.

---

# Cargo and Logistics

Not every R.A.M. is built primarily to destroy things.

Heavy logistical variants are capable of transporting ammunition, equipment, replacement components, medical supplies, and other battlefield cargo.

Their mechanical strength also allows them to move objects that would normally require dedicated engineering vehicles.

Logistics configurations may sacrifice portions of their weaponry and armor for:

- Cargo racks
- Manipulator systems
- Towing equipment
- Recovery equipment
- Ammunition storage
- Engineering tools

Even logistical R.A.M.s usually retain enough weaponry to defend themselves.

The Hell Hosts learned quickly that an unarmed cargo robot is simply an extremely expensive target.

---

# Armament

R.A.M. weapon configurations vary according to platform and mission.

Common weapon categories include:

### Heavy Ballistic Weapons

Large-caliber automatic cannons and machine guns provide sustained conventional firepower against infantry, light vehicles, and fortified positions.

Ballistic weapons remain common because of their reliability and enormous variety of available ammunition.

### Missile Systems

Missile pods allow R.A.M.s to engage vehicles, aircraft, fortified positions, and distant targets.

Different payloads may be installed depending upon operational requirements.

### Directed-Energy Weapons

Some platforms incorporate energy weapons for precision engagement, anti-armor fire, or situations where conventional ammunition supply is problematic.

Energy systems generally impose greater power and thermal requirements upon the machine.

### Defensive Systems

Many R.A.M.s also carry secondary defensive weapons intended to engage infantry attempting to approach the machine's vulnerable areas.

Larger platforms may possess multiple independently controlled weapon stations.

---

# Modular Construction

One of the major advantages of the R.A.M. program is modularity.

The same basic chassis may be reconfigured for radically different missions.

A machine operating as an assault platform during one campaign may later be rebuilt for defensive, engineering, or logistical work.

Replaceable components can include:

- Weapons
- Armor
- Sensor arrays
- Power systems
- Cargo modules
- Manipulator arms
- Missile racks
- Communications equipment
- Environmental protection systems

This modularity reduces the need to manufacture an entirely different machine for every battlefield role.

It also means damaged R.A.M.s can sometimes return to service using components recovered from other platforms.

---

# Infantry Cooperation

Hell Host doctrine discourages deploying R.A.M.s without infantry support unless circumstances specifically require independent operation.

Despite their durability, the machines possess vulnerabilities.

Enemy infantry can exploit terrain, blind spots, confined spaces, electronic warfare, mines, and specialized anti-armor weapons.

Organic personnel provide situational awareness and flexibility that heavy machines cannot always reproduce.

In return, R.A.M.s provide infantry with protection and firepower they cannot carry themselves.

Experienced formations learn to treat the machines less like oversized soldiers and more like **moving pieces of terrain**.

A R.A.M. can become cover.

A weapons platform.

A breaching tool.

A defensive wall.

Or something extremely large standing between the squad and whatever is trying to kill them.

---

# Hazardous Environments

R.A.M.s are commonly deployed into environments where conventional personnel would require extensive protective equipment.

Depending upon configuration, machines may operate in:

- High-radiation zones
- Toxic atmospheres
- Extreme temperatures
- Biological contamination zones
- Vacuum
- Severe storms
- Chemically hazardous environments

This makes them particularly useful for securing dangerous facilities or recovering equipment before organic personnel enter.

Machines exposed to anomalous environments are still subject to strict containment procedures.

Hell Host command does not assume that something is safe simply because it is mechanical.

---

# Maintenance

R.A.M.s require substantial logistical support.

Their armor, weapons, actuators, power systems, ammunition feeds, and sensor arrays undergo enormous strain during combat.

Dedicated engineering crews accompany major R.A.M. formations and are responsible for:

- Repair
- Rearmament
- Component replacement
- Software diagnostics
- Armor restoration
- Power-system maintenance
- Battlefield recovery

A badly damaged R.A.M. may appear completely destroyed while still containing recoverable components.

Hell Host recovery teams therefore salvage disabled machines whenever circumstances allow.

---

# Relationship with Titans

R.A.M.s should not be confused with [[Titans]].

Although both are large mechanized combat platforms, they occupy radically different strategic roles.

R.A.M.s are battlefield assets.

Titans are strategic war machines.

Even the heaviest conventional R.A.M. remains vastly smaller and less powerful than a true Hell Host Titan.

A useful distinction used by some Hell Host personnel is:

**If it supports the battle, it is probably a R.A.M.**

**If the battle is happening because it arrived, it is probably a Titan.**

---

# Relationship with Other Machines

The Hell Hosts field an enormous variety of robotic systems.

R.A.M.s represent only one category.

They differ significantly from individuals such as [[Enigma]] and [[Aeon Unit|Aegis]], whose sophisticated artificial intelligences and humanoid designs allow them to operate much more like individual soldiers.

A R.A.M. does not need to look human.

It does not need to behave human.

It needs to survive incoming fire, identify what is shooting at it, and make that problem stop.

---

# Reputation

R.A.M.s are generally popular among frontline Hell Host personnel.

Their arrival means additional armor, additional ammunition, additional sensors, and something considerably larger than a soldier for the enemy to shoot at.

Infantry have consequently developed numerous unofficial names for individual machines and chassis types.

Engineering personnel frequently object to these names.

This has never stopped anyone.

The machines themselves usually do not care.
