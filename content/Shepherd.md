# Shepherd

> *"I don't need you to like me. I need you to follow orders."*

## Profile

**Real Name:** Classified  
**Callsign:** Shepherd  
**Age:** Unknown  
**Gender:** Male  
**Species:** Human  
**Status:** Active  
**Faction:** [[Hell Hosts]]  
**Branch:** [[Doom Corps]]  
**Formation:** Shock and Awe Group  
**Rank:** Captain  
**Role:** Protective Operations / Combat Extraction Commander  
**Security Clearance:** Omega-Black  
**Threat Level:** High  
**Known Abilities:** None  
**Primary Weapon:** [[365 SPAR]]  
**Specialist Weapon:** [[Atrius IV]]  
**Secondary Weapon:** Combat Knife  

Shepherd is a Hell Host Captain specializing in protective operations, high-risk extraction, and battlefield command.

Unlike many of the anomalous or enhanced operatives serving within the [[Hell Hosts]], Shepherd possesses no known supernatural abilities, biological modifications, or anomalous powers.

He does not need them.

Shepherd has built his reputation through discipline, battlefield awareness, tactical experience, and an exceptional ability to bring personnel home from situations in which extraction should have been impossible.

He is most commonly deployed when high-value personnel must enter, operate within, or escape an active combat zone.

Senior officers.

Scientists.

Intelligence personnel.

Anomalous specialists.

Diplomats.

Critical witnesses.

Entire trapped units.

If their survival matters enough, Shepherd may be placed in charge of getting them out.

His philosophy is simple.

**The mission succeeds.**

**The protected personnel survive.**

**Everyone possible comes home.**

---

# Identity

Shepherd's real identity is highly classified.

Unlike anomalous individuals whose identities are unknown because of mysterious origins, Shepherd's identity is deliberately concealed.

He is human.

He has a past.

Hell Hosts knows who he is.

Most personnel simply do not have permission to know.

His personal records are restricted under Omega-Black clearance due to his extensive involvement in sensitive protective operations.

Shepherd has spent significant portions of his career around senior command personnel, classified intelligence, strategic assets, and individuals whose movements are themselves highly restricted.

Information concerning his civilian identity, family history, and early military service has therefore been removed from most accessible databases.

Shepherd rarely discusses the matter.

When addressed by personnel, **Shepherd is sufficient.**

---

# Shock and Awe Group

Shepherd serves within the **Shock and Awe Group**, a specialist Doom Corps formation focused on protective operations, high-intensity intervention, and combat extraction.

Despite its name, Shock and Awe is not primarily an offensive formation.

Its purpose is to create overwhelming local superiority long enough to protect or recover strategically important personnel.

The group's responsibilities include:

- High-risk personnel extraction
- Battlefield rescue
- VIP protection
- Combat-zone escort
- Emergency reinforcement
- Evacuation corridor creation
- Recovery of isolated personnel
- Protection of senior command
- Rapid-response intervention
- Hostile-area transport security

Shock and Awe doctrine differs considerably from conventional close protection.

When operating inside an active warzone, simply surrounding a protected individual with additional soldiers may accomplish very little.

Instead, Shock and Awe seeks to control the environment around them.

Enemy firing position threatening the evacuation route?

Destroy it.

Hostile armor blocking extraction?

Remove it.

Extraction zone compromised?

Create another.

Road unusable?

Find an aerial route.

Airspace contested?

Suppress it long enough to leave.

The objective is not subtlety.

The objective is survival.

The group's informal philosophy is reflected in its motto:

> **Protect. Serve. Ensure.**

# Role as Captain

As a Captain, Shepherd frequently commands protective detachments assembled specifically for individual operations.

The exact composition varies according to the mission.

A protection detail may include:

- Doom Corps infantry
- Armored vehicles
- Aircraft
- Reconnaissance personnel
- Medical teams
- Electronic warfare support
- Drone units
- Heavy weapons personnel
- Specialist operatives

Shepherd is comfortable commanding mixed formations and rapidly reorganizing available personnel when an operation deteriorates.

He is particularly effective when normal command structures have broken down.

If communications fail, leadership is lost, or the original extraction plan becomes impossible, Shepherd tends to become more dangerous rather than less effective.

He does not require the original plan to survive.

He requires the objective to survive.

---

# Command Philosophy

Shepherd is a demanding commander.

He expects orders to be followed quickly and accurately.

During combat, he has little patience for unnecessary arguments, hesitation, insubordination, or attempts at personal glory.

This has sometimes given him a reputation for being cold.

The description is incomplete.

Shepherd does not demand discipline because he enjoys authority.

He demands it because hesitation during an extraction gets people killed.

If Shepherd orders a position abandoned, there is a reason.

If he orders a unit to hold, there is a reason.

If he says an extraction window closes in thirty seconds, personnel are expected to be aboard in twenty-nine.

Shepherd rarely repeats himself.

> *"You can argue with me when we're home."*

---

# Acceptable Losses

Shepherd's protective philosophy does not mean he believes every casualty can always be prevented.

He understands the mathematics of warfare.

There are situations in which personnel must hold a position so others can escape.

There are vehicles that cannot be recovered.

There are wounded who cannot safely be reached.

There are extraction routes that remain open only because someone stays behind to defend them.

Shepherd is capable of giving those orders.

He does not give them casually.

When forced to choose between losing a small force and allowing an entire operation to collapse, Shepherd will make the decision he believes preserves the greatest number of lives while completing the objective.

This pragmatism has occasionally placed him in conflict with other commanders.

Shepherd does not enjoy sacrificing personnel.

He simply refuses to pretend war will always give him another option.

# The Hell Host-Free World War

Shepherd earned much of his modern reputation during the [[Hell Host-Free World War]].

The conflict repeatedly required Hell Host forces to operate across unstable fronts, contested cities, damaged installations, and territory threatened by [[Free World Order]] forces.

Shepherd participated in numerous protection and extraction operations during the war.

His assignments frequently involved recovering isolated Hell Host personnel and protecting officers operating near active combat zones.

Several operations credited to Shock and Awe involved evacuation routes that had already been considered lost.

Shepherd's effectiveness during the conflict brought him into increasing contact with senior Hell Host command.

Older records sometimes summarize his objective simply as:

**Defeat the Free World.**

Modern documentation considers this an oversimplification of his wartime role.

Shepherd's priority was never ideological hatred of the enemy.

His priority was ensuring Hell Host personnel survived them.

# Protective Operations

Shepherd approaches protective work differently from conventional bodyguards.

He does not remain permanently beside the protected individual unless circumstances require it.

Instead, he studies the entire operational environment.

He considers:

- Enemy positions
- Terrain
- Civilian presence
- Available cover
- Medical support
- Airspace
- Vehicle routes
- Communications
- Alternate extraction zones
- Friendly reinforcement times
- Potential ambush locations
- Structural hazards
- Enemy response patterns

Shepherd attempts to solve threats before they physically reach the person he is protecting.

When that fails, Shock and Awe doctrine shifts rapidly toward overwhelming force.

---

# Extraction Doctrine

Shepherd rarely relies upon a single extraction plan.

Operations under his command typically possess several contingencies.

**Primary extraction fails.**

Move to secondary.

**Secondary route is compromised.**

Create a new route.

**Aircraft cannot land.**

Move the extraction point.

**Vehicles are destroyed.**

Proceed on foot.

**Communications fail.**

Continue according to contingency doctrine.

Shepherd considers an extraction plan that depends upon everything going correctly to be no plan at all.

His ability to rapidly improvise alternatives is one of the primary reasons he is trusted with strategically important personnel.

---

# Combat Capability

Shepherd is an exceptionally capable conventional soldier.

He possesses no known powers.

His effectiveness comes from:

- Extensive combat experience
- Physical conditioning
- Weapons proficiency
- Tactical awareness
- Situational discipline
- Vehicle experience
- Command ability
- Rapid decision-making

Shepherd is physically strong and capable in close combat, but does not seek personal engagements unnecessarily.

His responsibility as a commander takes priority over individual kill counts.

If another soldier can make the shot while Shepherd manages the wider battle, he lets them make it.

If Shepherd needs to make the shot himself, he will.

---

# 365 SPAR

Shepherd's standard service weapon is the [[365 SPAR]].

The rifle's versatility, reliability, and hybrid combat capability make it well suited to protection operations where the exact nature of the threat may change rapidly.

Shepherd favors dependable equipment over experimental systems.

A weapon carried during an extraction must function when required.

He has little interest in equipment that performs spectacularly under ideal conditions but becomes unreliable when those conditions disappear.

---

# Atrius IV

For operations expected to involve heavy armor, fortified positions, or extreme resistance, Shepherd may deploy with an [[Atrius IV]] heavy charge pulse rifle.

The Atrius IV is not part of his normal patrol loadout.

It is brought when intelligence indicates that Shock and Awe may require substantially greater firepower.

The weapon's ability to deliver devastating charged energy pulses makes it particularly effective when Shepherd needs to eliminate a threat blocking an extraction corridor.

Among personnel serving under him, the appearance of an Atrius IV in Shepherd's loadout is generally interpreted as a warning.

The mission is expected to become unpleasant.

---

# Combat Knife

Shepherd carries a conventional combat knife.

It possesses no anomalous properties.

It does not contain an experimental energy field.

It does not distort reality.

It is a knife.

Shepherd considers this sufficient.

---

# Vehicle Experience

Shepherd possesses considerable experience operating both ground vehicles and aircraft.

He is not primarily a pilot or vehicle specialist, but maintains sufficient proficiency to assume direct control during emergency situations.

This is particularly valuable during extraction operations.

If the designated driver or pilot is incapacitated, Shepherd does not intend to remain stranded simply because nobody else can operate the vehicle.

He has experience with:

- Armored transports
- Utility vehicles
- Light combat vehicles
- Atmospheric aircraft
- Military transports

His proficiency varies between platforms.

He does not claim to be an expert in everything with an engine.

---

# Personality

Shepherd is usually quiet, stern, and controlled.

He does not speak simply to fill silence.

Around unfamiliar personnel, he can appear cold and intensely professional.

He expects discipline and has little tolerance for avoidable mistakes during active operations.

However, personnel who earn Shepherd's trust encounter a noticeably different side of him.

He can be approachable.

He possesses a dry sense of humor.

He listens to his soldiers.

He remembers personal details.

He checks on wounded personnel after operations.

Most importantly, he is fiercely protective of people he considers his responsibility.

Shepherd does not become dramatically affectionate.

He simply makes it increasingly difficult for anything to kill them.

---

# Trust

Shepherd does not trust easily.

Once that trust has been earned, however, it is exceptionally difficult to lose without serious cause.

Personnel under his command often describe him as easier to trust than to know.

He does not promise that everyone will survive.

He considers such promises dishonest.

Instead, Shepherd offers something considerably more practical:

He will do everything reasonably possible to bring them home.

And if he orders someone into danger, he will never pretend he does not understand what he is asking.

---

# The Fallen

Shepherd maintains private records of personnel killed during operations under his command.

The practice is not required by Hell Host regulation.

He has never publicly explained why he does it.

Personnel close to Shepherd believe he considers remembering the dead part of the responsibility of command.

If he must order someone into a position from which they may not return, he refuses to allow them to become merely another casualty statistic afterward.

Shepherd rarely discusses the records.

He never deletes them.

---

# Relationship with [[Predator]]

Shepherd has a strong professional relationship with [[Predator]].

His reputation for protection and extraction has resulted in repeated assignments involving senior Hell Host command.

Predator trusts Shepherd's battlefield judgement considerably.

This becomes particularly noticeable during emergency extraction situations.

Shepherd is one of the relatively few officers willing to directly tell Predator that an operation has reached the point where remaining on site serves no useful purpose.

Predator does not always appreciate hearing this.

He usually listens.

Their relationship is built less upon personal obedience and more upon professional trust.

When Shepherd assumes control of an extraction, Predator understands that his decisions are being made for a reason.

> **Shepherd:** "We're leaving."

> **Predator:** "I'm not finished."

> **Shepherd:** "The enemy disagrees."

---

# Relationship with [[Dreadnought]]

[[Dreadnought]] respects Shepherd's discipline and command ability.

The two occasionally disagree over operational priorities.

As a fleet commander, Dreadnought must consider the strategic consequences of an operation across an entire theater.

Shepherd's responsibility may be considerably narrower:

**Get these people out alive.**

This difference can produce disagreements regarding acceptable losses, resource allocation, and how much military force should be committed to individual extraction operations.

Neither considers the other incompetent.

They simply approach warfare from different levels of command.

---

# Relationship with [[Scyther]]

Shepherd and [[Scyther]] share strong professional respect.

Both understand the importance of completing an objective under catastrophic conditions.

Shepherd values Scyther's experience, adaptability, and ability to survive environments that would kill conventional personnel.

Scyther, in turn, respects Shepherd's refusal to panic when an operation begins collapsing.

Shepherd has participated in operations where Scyther's anomalous capabilities represented both a major asset and a potential operational complication.

He treats both facts seriously.

---

# Relationship with [[Infinity]]

Shepherd considers [[Infinity]] exceptionally valuable during protection operations.

Her speed and Danger Sense make her highly effective as a rapid interception asset.

When deployed together, Infinity is frequently positioned away from the principal rather than directly beside them.

If a threat emerges, her role is to reach it before it reaches the protected personnel.

Shepherd appreciates her effectiveness.

Her unpredictability occasionally irritates him.

Infinity appears to consider this funny.

---

# Relationship with [[B.O.B]]

Shepherd works extremely well with [[B.O.B]].

Protective operations depend heavily upon rapid access to reliable battlefield information, making B.O.B an ideal support intelligence for Shepherd's command style.

B.O.B can provide:

- Threat analysis
- Extraction route updates
- Friendly-force positions
- Vehicle availability
- Medical status
- Drone reconnaissance
- Enemy movement predictions

Unlike some commanders, Shepherd rarely ignores B.O.B's warnings simply because they conflict with his preferred plan.

If new information makes the plan obsolete, Shepherd changes the plan.

B.O.B approves of this.

Probably more enthusiastically than he admits.

---

# Languages

Shepherd is multilingual.

Known languages include:

- English
- German
- Spanish
- Basic Chinese

His language skills are particularly useful during multinational or civilian-heavy protection operations.

He does not consider basic proficiency equivalent to fluency and has corrected personnel who exaggerated his abilities.

---

# Reputation

Shepherd is not among the most publicly celebrated members of the Hell Hosts.

That suits him.

His work frequently occurs around people who receive considerably more attention than he does.

Senior commanders.

Important researchers.

Anomalous personnel.

Strategic specialists.

His success is measured differently.

The protected officer boards the transport.

The trapped squad reaches friendly lines.

The scientist leaves the collapsing facility.

The wounded are evacuated.

The aircraft takes off.

The door closes.

Everyone who could be saved is aboard.

Only then does Shepherd consider leaving.

---

# Philosophy

Shepherd's callsign has produced obvious comparisons throughout his career.

He does not particularly care for them.

Nevertheless, the comparison fits.

A shepherd's purpose is not to be loved by the flock.

It is not to be admired.

It is not to make the journey comfortable.

It is to guide them through dangerous ground and ensure they reach the other side.

Shepherd's methods can be harsh.

His orders can be demanding.

His decisions can be difficult.

But beneath all of them is the same objective.

**Get them home.**

> *"I don't promise you'll be safe. I promise I'll give you a way out."*
