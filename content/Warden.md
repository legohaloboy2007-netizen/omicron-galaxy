Overview

Jack Vandrix, known throughout the [[Hell Hosts]] as Warden, is a veteran commander of the [[Doom Corps]] and one of the most respected living members of [[House Vandrix]].

As the eldest of the Vandrix siblings, Warden helped establish the family’s modern military reputation through discipline, long service, battlefield reliability, and unbreakable standards.

He is not the loudest commander in the Hell Hosts.

He is one of the most trusted.

Forged across decades of brutal campaigns, Warden earned his rank through results rather than politics. He became known for stabilising failing fronts, reorganising broken battalions, and holding defensive lines that others had already written off as lost.

Among soldiers, he is feared for his standards.

Among veterans, he is trusted because those standards keep people alive.

A common phrase associated with him states:

«“Many soldiers fear serving under him. Most request to stay.”»

---

House Vandrix

[[House Vandrix]] is one of the old military families whose history predates the modern Hell Hosts.

The family is known for:

- Discipline
- Duty
- Reliability
- Command
- Tradition
- Service
- Battlefield endurance
- Professionalism under pressure

House Vandrix is not known for producing reckless heroes or theatrical warriors.

It is known for producing dependable soldiers, officers, and commanders who can be trusted when a battle begins to collapse.

Within the Hell Hosts, the Vandrix name carries expectation.

To bear it is to inherit responsibility before personal reputation.

A common belief within the family states:

«“Your name reaches the battlefield before you do.”»

For Warden, this is not a point of pride alone.

It is a burden.

---

Family

Warden is the eldest of the Vandrix siblings.

Known members of House Vandrix include:

- [[Doom Vandrix]]
- [[Killer Vandrix]]
- [[Hope Vandrix]]
- [[War Vandrix]]
- [[Red Vandrix]]

As the eldest, Warden carries the family legacy heavily.

He is not merely a senior officer. He is one of the figures responsible for ensuring that House Vandrix remains associated with discipline, stability, and honour rather than arrogance or entitlement.

Warden also has children of his own.

Although he remains active within the Doom Corps, his family has changed the way he views war. He still deploys when necessary, but he spends less time personally entering combat than he once did.

He has something to come home to now.

That does not make him softer.

It makes every life under his command feel more real.

---

Personality

Warden is strict, quiet, deeply observant, practical, and difficult to impress.

He rarely wastes words, and his silence is often more intimidating than another commander’s anger.

He is not naturally charismatic in the traditional sense. He does not inspire through grand speeches or dramatic gestures. Instead, soldiers follow him because his orders make sense, his standards are consistent, and his discipline has saved lives.

Common traits include:

- Strict
- Quiet
- Highly observant
- Practical
- Intolerant of excuses
- Rewards competence
- Privately compassionate
- Difficult to sway
- Deeply disciplined
- Emotionally restrained
- Loyal to his soldiers
- Reluctant to show vulnerability

Warden does not openly display affection or sentimentality.

But those who know him well understand that he cares deeply.

He simply shows it through preparation, supply checks, rotation schedules, battlefield planning, and making sure soldiers are not wasted for pride.

---

Biggest Flaw

Warden refuses to retire.

Despite his age, long service, family responsibilities, and decades of accumulated strain, he continues to serve actively within the Doom Corps.

Doctors, officers, and even old comrades have advised him to step back.

Warden always gives the same answer:

«“Not yet.”»

His refusal does not come from vanity.

It comes from responsibility.

He believes there are still soldiers who need experienced commanders, fronts that require discipline, and younger officers who are not ready to carry the weight alone.

This makes him valuable.

It also makes him dangerously unwilling to admit when his own service is costing him too much.

---

Leadership Style

Warden believes soldiers follow consistency more than charisma.

He expects discipline, but never without purpose. His standards are high because he believes poor discipline gets people killed.

Under Warden’s command, soldiers are expected to:

- Maintain their equipment
- Follow orders precisely
- Know their roles
- Respect formation discipline
- Report honestly
- Protect civilians when ordered
- Avoid unnecessary risks
- Hold positions unless withdrawal is ordered
- Take responsibility for failures
- Improve after correction

Warden does not tolerate excuses.

However, he also does not waste soldiers carelessly.

He ensures his troops are equipped, trained, supplied, and rotated when possible. He expects officers under him to know the condition of their soldiers rather than treat them as numbers.

To Warden, command is not about appearing powerful.

It is about being responsible when everyone else begins to panic.

One of his most repeated beliefs is:

«“Panic is contagious. So is discipline.”»

---

Command Reputation

Warden’s command reputation was built across decades of difficult campaigns.

He became known for entering situations where:

- Frontlines were collapsing
- Battalions had lost cohesion
- Officers had been killed
- Supply chains were failing
- Morale was breaking
- Enemy pressure was overwhelming
- Retreat seemed inevitable

Warden’s arrival rarely meant an easy victory.

It meant the line would stop falling apart.

He reorganised shattered forces, reassigned surviving officers, restored communication, reinforced weak points, and forced panic back into discipline.

Among officers, Warden is considered almost impossible to sway once he has made a tactical assessment.

If Warden says a line can hold, most believe it can.

If Warden says a plan will fail, most listen.

---

The 13th Ironwall Battalion

Before becoming a senior Doom Corps commander, Warden served with and rose through the [[13th Ironwall Battalion]].

The 13th Ironwall became known for one defining doctrine:

«Hold impossible positions.»

The battalion specialised in defensive warfare, line stabilisation, attrition survival, and delaying actions against superior forces.

It was not the flashiest battalion in the Doom Corps.

It was one of the most dependable.

The 13th Ironwall shaped Warden’s entire view of command. He learned that battles are not always won by breaking the enemy first. Sometimes, victory comes from refusing to collapse long enough for the rest of the army to recover.

Even after rising far beyond the battalion, Warden quietly maintains a deep attachment to the 13th Ironwall.

It was his first true military home.

---

Combat Role

Although Warden is primarily a command-level officer, he still deploys personally when fronts collapse or morale fails.

His presence on the battlefield is usually treated as a serious sign.

If Warden is present, the situation has either become critical or is important enough to require direct senior oversight.

He typically fights with disciplined, practical efficiency rather than spectacle.

His usual weapons include:

- [[CR-556 Combat Rifle]]
- [[PX20 Service Pistol]]
- [[A12 Heavy Shotgun]]

When Warden personally carries the [[A12 Heavy Shotgun]], troops understand that the situation has become severe.

It usually means close combat is expected, the line is unstable, or Warden intends to personally help break an enemy push.

Weapons

CR-556 Combat Rifle

The [[CR-556 Combat Rifle]] is Warden’s standard battlefield weapon.

Reliable, accurate, and practical, it reflects his personality almost perfectly.

Warden favours weapons that function under pressure and do not require theatrics to be effective.

---

PX20 Service Pistol

The [[PX20 Service Pistol]] is Warden’s standard sidearm.

His model is customised for precision, though not excessively decorated.

Like most of Warden’s equipment, it is maintained with near-obsessive care.

---

A12 Heavy Shotgun

The [[A12 Heavy Shotgun]] is Warden’s close-quarters weapon.

He does not carry it for intimidation.

He carries it when something needs to end quickly.

Among Doom Corps soldiers, seeing Warden take up the A12 is considered a warning that the battle has reached a dangerous point.

Awards and Decorations

Warden has received several major military awards throughout his career.

Known decorations include:

- [[Founder's Commendation]]
- [[Iron Wall Citation]]
- [[Defense of Voridinus Medal]]
- [[Commonwealth Campaign Star]]
- [[Distinguished Command Cross]]
- [[Valor Under Fire Medal]]

Warden does not often discuss his medals.

He views them as records of responsibility rather than personal glory.

Relationship with Predator

Warden and [[Predator]] share a relationship of long-standing respect.

They have fought together across multiple eras of Hell Host history, and their trust was earned through years of service rather than ceremony.

Predator respects Warden because he is reliable, disciplined, and almost impossible to corrupt through ambition.

Warden respects Predator because he understands the burden of command at the highest level.

Their conversations are often brief and direct.

There are few officers who can speak plainly to Predator without fear.

Warden is one of them.

In private, Predator may refer to him simply as Jack.

Warden may answer him as William.

No titles are needed between soldiers who have survived enough wars together.

---

Relationship with Bulwark

Warden and [[Bulwark]] share immense mutual respect.

Bulwark commands the Doom Corps as a whole, while Warden represents one of its most reliable senior field commanders.

Their leadership styles differ, but they complement one another.

Bulwark is often seen as the commander who understands the soldiers.

Warden is seen as the commander who understands the line.

Both care deeply about the troops under their command, though they express it differently.

Bulwark may sit with a soldier and listen.

Warden may never say a comforting word, but he will quietly adjust a deployment schedule, fix a supply problem, or remove an incompetent officer before more damage is done.

Neither mistakes the other’s silence for indifference.

---

Relationship with Champion

Warden respects [[Champion]] as a capable battlefield leader and one of the most important figures in the Hell Hosts.

Champion’s command style is more openly inspiring than Warden’s, but Warden does not consider that a weakness. He recognises Champion’s ability to motivate soldiers and win trust in the field.

Their relationship is professional, occasionally tense, but ultimately respectful.

Champion may lead charges.

Warden makes sure there is still a line for those charges to return to.

---

Relationship with Destiny

Warden’s relationship with [[Destiny]] is shaped by discipline and expectation.

He does not treat her lightly because she is Predator’s daughter.

If anything, he expects more from her because of it.

During training or early service, Destiny may have found Warden frustrating, harsh, or impossible to satisfy. His corrections are blunt, and his approval is rare.

Over time, however, she would likely come to understand that his standards were never personal.

Warden expects excellence because he believes soldiers are capable of it.

He does not flatter Destiny.

He prepares her.

---

Relationship with Red Wind

Warden does not always know how to comfort people directly, especially those carrying emotional wounds as deep as [[Red Wind]].

He is not the kind of officer to sit down and gently talk through pain.

Instead, Warden helps in quieter ways.

If Red Wind is exhausted, he may adjust her deployment rotation.

If she is overwhelmed, he may move unnecessary personnel away from her.

If her unit is struggling, he may ensure supplies arrive early.

He rarely explains these decisions.

He simply makes them.

Red Wind may never know how often Warden has quietly protected her from behind a wall of paperwork and command authority.

---

Relationship with House Vandrix

Warden’s relationship with House Vandrix is complicated by responsibility.

As the eldest sibling and one of the most respected living members of the family, he carries the burden of setting the standard for those who follow.

To Warden, House Vandrix must never become a name used for entitlement.

It must remain a name associated with service.

He expects his siblings and children to earn respect rather than inherit it.

This has made him a difficult figure within his own family at times, but also one of its strongest pillars.

---

Relationship with House Rhenford

[[House Rhenford]] is another old family whose history predates the modern Hell Hosts.

Where House Vandrix is associated with discipline, duty, and holding the line, House Rhenford is often associated with innovation, adaptability, specialist warfare, and breaking through problems that tradition cannot solve.

The two houses are not necessarily enemies.

They are counterparts.

House Vandrix| House Rhenford
Discipline| Innovation
Duty| Adaptability
Commanders| Specialists
Hold the line| Break the line
Tradition| Progress
Stability| Improvisation

Warden respects House Rhenford when its members prove competent.

However, he has little patience for reckless innovation without discipline.

To Warden, breaking the line is valuable only if someone is still there to hold what remains.

Office

Warden’s office reflects his personality.

It is clean, organised, and almost unnervingly precise.

Common features include:

- Perfectly stacked reports
- A clean desk
- A House Vandrix banner
- A Doom Corps banner
- A family photograph
- An old helmet from the 13th Ironwall Battalion
- A locked cabinet of campaign files
- A few carefully maintained medals
- A Blackstar mug
- A clock set five minutes fast

Warden drinks [[Reactor Blend]] rather than standard coffee.

When asked why, his answer is usually:

«“Because it works.”»

The office clock is always five minutes fast.

When asked why, he once replied:

«“Late starts become failed operations.”»

---

Habits

Warden maintains several habits that have become known among those who serve under him.

Most mornings, he personally walks through at least one barracks, training hall, command post, or equipment bay.

He does this without an entourage.

No speeches.

No ceremony.

He looks.

He listens.

He checks details others miss.

To Warden, commanders should occasionally see the people they command without filters, reports, or staged inspections.

Soldiers dread these walks.

They also trust them.

Problems discovered by Warden tend to be fixed quickly.

---

Reputation

Among Troops

Warden is viewed as harsh, strict, and demanding.

He is not usually described as friendly.

However, many soldiers trust him deeply because his standards are consistent and his decisions are rarely careless.

A common description among troops is:

«“Hard man. Fair standards.”»

Among Officers

Among officers, Warden is respected as a commander who cannot be easily manipulated, impressed, or intimidated.

His judgement is treated seriously, especially in matters involving frontline stability, defensive operations, and battalion recovery.

Among Enemies

Among enemies, Warden’s arrival carries a simple message:

«The line just stopped moving.»

Forces expecting a collapsing front often find themselves facing renewed discipline, reorganised defences, and a commander who refuses to yield ground without making them pay for every metre.

---

Legacy

Warden’s legacy is not built on spectacle.

It is built on consistency.

He has spent decades proving that discipline, preparation, and responsibility can turn broken fronts into survivable battlefields.

He does not seek to be remembered as the greatest warrior in Hell Host history.

If remembered at all, he would prefer to be remembered as a commander whose soldiers had a better chance of coming home because he was there.

For House Vandrix, he represents the family’s modern standard.

For the Doom Corps, he represents the officer every commander is expected to become.
